# Lifespan, accessibility, and safety

Age changes developmental context, authority, communication, pacing, and safe interface choices; it is not a mastery score. Within-age variation is large, so pair age-informed defaults with direct observation.

## Lifespan defaults

| Context | Default role of the system | Design emphasis | Hard caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infant / under 2 | Coach the caregiver for talk, turn-taking, songs, naming, movement, and shared attention | Real-world interaction; extremely brief optional shared media | Do not design a solitary screen tutor or collect child interaction traces by default |
| Ages 2–5 | Caregiver-mediated stories, play prompts, creation, conversation, and physical-world tasks | High-quality content, co-use, short sessions, language-rich play | Screen use must not displace sleep, movement, relationships, or hands-on exploration |
| Ages 6–12 | Interactive tutor under guardian controls | Concrete-to-abstract examples, visible goals, short loops, curiosity, safe autonomy, teach learning strategies explicitly | Avoid manipulative engagement, public sharing, open-ended external contact, or hidden profiling |
| Ages 13–17 | Collaborative coach with growing learner agency and age-appropriate guardian/safeguarding controls | Identity-safe choice, metacognition, authentic projects, critical AI literacy | Do not confuse apparent maturity with adult consent or remove escalation paths |
| Adult | Learner-directed coach | Goals, prior experience, relevance, efficient diagnostics, self-regulation | Do not assume expertise transfers across domains or that all adults are digitally/accessibly alike |

WHO guidance does not recommend sedentary screen time for infants and one-year-olds and limits it for ages 2–4; AAP guidance similarly emphasizes avoiding most media under 18 months and co-viewing high-quality content for young children. These are health/development guardrails, not proof that all digital learning is ineffective. [S014](SOURCES.md#s014), [S030](SOURCES.md#s030).

## Accessibility is capability, not style

Apply Universal Design for Learning as a barrier-removal framework, then verify the specific implementation:

- multiple accessible ways to perceive essential information;
- multiple compatible ways to act and express knowledge;
- adjustable pace, complexity, sensory load, text, captions, audio, contrast, motion, and input method;
- preserved learning objective when an irrelevant access demand is removed;
- explicit learner control and an escape from adaptations that do not help.

Do not lower the mastery target when the accommodation changes only the access channel. When the target itself is modality-specific (for example, French listening or pronunciation), measure that capability directly and provide other access support around it.

## Disability and neurodiversity workflow

1. Ask what task or interface barrier exists; do not demand a diagnosis.
2. Offer reversible accommodations grounded in accessibility practice.
3. Observe access errors separately from target-skill errors.
4. Retain the learner's stated preference and correction authority.
5. Escalate high-stakes, clinical, or safeguarding questions to an authorized human.

## Child-centered AI requirements

- best interests and developmental well-being before engagement or data growth;
- age-appropriate transparency: the learner knows it is an AI and when it is uncertain;
- guardian controls appropriate to age without denying the child's voice;
- no advertising, persuasive retention loops, social comparison, or monetization of learning data;
- grounded content, bounded generation, reporting, and human escalation;
- red-team unsafe content, grooming-like interaction, dependency cues, bias, and disclosure elicitation;
- minimize, isolate, expire, export, correct, and delete learner data.

UNICEF treats privacy, fairness, safety, transparency, inclusion, and child well-being as system-level AI requirements. COPPA obligations can apply to child-directed services and reinforce parental consent, data minimization, and retention limits; exact legal applicability requires jurisdiction-specific review. [S013](SOURCES.md#s013), [S015](SOURCES.md#s015).
