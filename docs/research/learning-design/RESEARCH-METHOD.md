# Research method and limits

## Question

Which evidence and rights/safety constraints should shape an adaptive agentic tutor intended to reduce time to demonstrated mastery across ages, abilities, disabilities, and domains, with an initial age-11 French use case?

## Source strategy

This first-pass synthesis (searched through 2026-08-03) prioritized:

1. systematic reviews and meta-analyses of learning interventions;
2. controlled and randomized studies for representative mechanisms and recent AI tutoring;
3. official frameworks/guidance from the Council of Europe, CAST, WHO, UNICEF, AAP, and FTC;
4. technical surveys for learner modeling;
5. recent evidence only when older syntheses cannot address generative AI.

Claims were narrowed to the population/outcome visible in the source. The corpus avoids numeric effect-size comparison across incompatible controls, measures, ages, subjects, and follow-up periods.

## Important limits

- This is a design synthesis, not a registered systematic review; database coverage, screening, bias appraisal, and duplicate-study correction are incomplete.
- Many classic learning findings originate in controlled or older settings and require field validation in this product.
- Educational flow evidence is substantially correlational; flow cannot be treated as a causal learning mechanism or primary KPI.
- Generative-tutor evidence is recent, heterogeneous, and narrow. Model, prompt, curriculum, human support, and assessment design change the intervention.
- UDL and rights/regulatory guidance supply valuable design constraints but do not by themselves prove learning gains.
- “Children,” “disabled learners,” “neurodivergent learners,” and age bands are heterogeneous; user involvement and subgroup validation are required.
- COPPA is U.S.-specific and this corpus is not legal advice. Production needs jurisdiction and deployment review.
- The age-11 French scenario is synthetic and cannot substitute for onboarding or learner/guardian input.

## Update triggers

Re-research a claim when a design applies it outside its supported population/outcome, a source is retracted/superseded, a safety or legal context changes, product evidence conflicts, or a new high-quality synthesis materially changes the grade.

## Evidence debt

Highest-priority gaps for later review:

- causal interventions that deliberately create healthy learning flow;
- long-term, unaided, transfer-focused outcomes for generative tutors in children;
- inclusive learner-model calibration with sparse multi-domain data;
- privacy-preserving cross-user learning analytics with useful small-cohort signals;
- evidence for caregiver-mediated AI support in early childhood;
- adaptive French instruction for young learners across pronunciation, interaction, literacy, and accessibility needs.
