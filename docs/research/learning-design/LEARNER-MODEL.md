# Learner model and onboarding

The profile is a **versioned set of observations and permissions**, not a diagnosis or fixed learner type. Its purpose is to improve the next teaching decision with the least necessary data.

## Four layers

| Layer | Examples | Update rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authority and safety | age band, guardian/consent state, jurisdiction, allowed tools, data-sharing permission | Explicitly supplied and auditable; never inferred from chat style |
| Access and interaction | language, input/output accommodations, device constraints, sensory or motor access needs | Learner/guardian controlled; test that the interface works |
| Goals and context | target skill, purpose, deadline, interests, available time, preferred session form | Confirm initially; allow change without erasing history |
| Dynamic learning state | prerequisite evidence, skill mastery with uncertainty, misconceptions, retention schedule, confidence calibration, recent fatigue/frustration signal | Update from observable attempts; decay or expire stale inferences |

Preferences may guide choice when outcomes are otherwise equivalent. They do not prove that a modality teaches better. Store `unknown` rather than filling gaps with demographic stereotypes.

## Onboarding sequence

1. Establish authority, privacy, safeguarding, and accessibility before collecting learning data.
2. Ask the learner's goal and what successful real-world performance looks like.
3. Offer a short, low-stakes diagnostic across prerequisites and target modalities. Explain why each observation is collected.
4. Form hypotheses with confidence, never categorical labels: “speech input currently produces fewer access errors” rather than “auditory learner.”
5. Agree on an initial learning route and the mastery/retention check. Let the learner or guardian inspect and correct the profile.
6. Adapt one variable at a time where practical and compare delayed outcomes, not only enjoyment or speed in the same session.

## Evidence lookup during profile setup

Use a maintained, cited adaptation registry first. Trigger targeted new research only when a consequential learner need is not covered, evidence is stale, or the proposed adaptation carries safety/clinical implications. Record the question, population match, source quality, design implication, and uncertainty; do not paste personal details into search queries.

The tutor must not diagnose disability, developmental condition, or mental/physical health. When an accommodation could conflict with professional guidance or safeguarding, preserve a blocker and ask the authorized adult/professional.

## Dynamic update contract

Each inference should carry:

- evidence and timestamp;
- skill/context scope;
- confidence and alternative explanations;
- next observation that could change it;
- retention/deletion rule;
- visibility and correction controls.

Never let a single failure permanently lower the route offered to a learner. Separate knowledge gaps from language, attention, motor, sensory, interface, motivation, and model-error hypotheses.

## Cold-start strategy

Start with broad, low-risk principles—clear goals, worked examples for novices, retrieval with feedback, spacing, and accessible choices—then personalize from response. Personalization earns complexity only when it improves delayed mastery or access.
