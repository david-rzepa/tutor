# Source registry

Source IDs keep principle files compact. Each entry records why it is in the corpus; agents should consult the original before making a high-stakes or population-specific claim.

## S001

Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan, and Willingham (2013), “Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques.” Broad evidence review; rates practice testing and distributed practice highly while emphasizing conditions and population limits. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)

## S002

Roediger and Karpicke (2006), “Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention.” Controlled experiments comparing repeated study with retrieval across immediate and delayed tests. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)

## S003

Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork (2008), “Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence.” Review specifies the evidence required for the meshing hypothesis and finds no adequate basis for routine learning-style matching. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) · [Open record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/)

## S004

Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, and Rohrer (2006), “Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis.” Meta-analysis of spacing and retention intervals. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)

## S005

Brunmair and Richter (2019), “Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators.” Synthesis of 59 studies showing material similarity and other moderators matter. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/)

## S006

Schwonke et al. (2009), “The worked-example effect: Not an artefact of lousy control conditions.” Controlled evidence for worked examples even against supported problem solving, interpreted with the wider cognitive-load literature. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563208002161)

## S007

“Supporting learning from text: A meta-analysis on the timing and content of effective feedback” (2020). Meta-analysis spanning primary school through university and examining timing/content moderators. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X1930154X)

## S008

Kulik, Kulik, and Bangert-Drowns (1990), “Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis.” Synthesis of 108 controlled evaluations across upper elementary, secondary, and college settings. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)

## S009

Council of Europe (2020), “Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching, assessment—Companion volume.” Official outcome/descriptor framework adding Pre-A1, mediation, online interaction, phonological and plurilingual/pluricultural competence. [Official portal](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/home)

## S010

CAST (2024), “Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0.” Research-informed barrier-removal and learner-agency framework; use as inclusive design guidance, not as proof that every implementation improves mastery. [Official guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/)

## S011

Dignath, Buettner, and Langfeldt (2008), “How can primary school students learn self-regulated learning strategies most effectively?” Meta-analysis of 48 treatment comparisons from 30 articles. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X08000080)

## S012

Wang et al. (2024), “A systematic review and meta-analysis of self-determination-theory-based interventions in the education context.” Synthesizes 36 interventions targeting autonomy, competence, relatedness, and autonomous motivation. [Author-hosted paper](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/2024_WangWangEtAl_MetaEdu.pdf)

## S013

UNICEF Innocenti (2025), “Guidance on AI and Children 3.0.” Rights-based requirements for child-centered AI, including privacy, fairness, safety, transparency, inclusion, and well-being. [Official guidance](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children)

## S014

World Health Organization (2019), “Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age.” Authoritative health guidance covering sedentary screen time alongside movement and sleep. [Official publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550536)

## S015

U.S. Federal Trade Commission, “Complying with COPPA: Frequently Asked Questions,” plus the amended-rule summary. Primary regulatory guidance on child-directed services, parental consent, data minimization, deletion, and retention. [FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) · [2025 rule update](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)

## S016

Alfredo et al. (2024), “Human-centred learning analytics and AI in education: A systematic literature review.” Reviews 108 papers and identifies limited end-user involvement plus gaps around control, safety, reliability, and trust. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2024.100215)

## S018

Liu et al. (2024), “Knowledge Tracing: A Survey.” Technical survey of knowledge-tracing models, datasets, capabilities, and limitations; supports probabilistic, inspectable learner-state design rather than naive correctness counts. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3569576)

## S019

Matayoshi et al. (2025), “Using a Randomized Experiment to Compare Mastery Learning Thresholds.” Directly tests two adaptive-system thresholds and finds retention/forgetting tradeoffs change over time. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15698758)

## S020

Bastani et al. (2025), “Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics.” Preregistered field experiment comparing standard GPT-4 access, a guarded tutor, and control, with unaided exam outcomes. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)

## S021

Wang et al. (2024), “Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise.” Randomized field trial of AI support for live K–12 tutors; useful evidence for human-AI complementarity, not autonomous replacement. [Stanford research page](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)

## S022

Kestin et al. (2025), “AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting.” College physics RCT; promising but narrow and not directly generalizable to children or other domains. [Open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)

## S023

Lyster, Saito, and Sato (2013), “Oral corrective feedback in second language classrooms.” Research synthesis covering feedback forms, preferences, contexts, and learner variables. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/abs/oral-corrective-feedback-in-second-language-classrooms/B33FC71C12A1317DCA0CE704B7B0BC00)

## S024

Li (2010), “The Effectiveness of Corrective Feedback in SLA: A Meta-Analysis.” Synthesizes 33 primary studies and supports feedback while documenting treatment/context moderators. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x)

## S025

Bryfonski and McKay (2019), “TBLT implementation and evaluation: A meta-analysis.” Research synthesis on task-based language teaching and implementation variation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168817744389)

## S026

Liu and Khalil (2023), “Understanding Privacy and Data Protection Issues in Learning Analytics Using a Systematic Review.” Review of 47 papers finding privacy issues persist across the analytics lifecycle. [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1395309)

## S027

UNICEF, “Governance of student data.” Rights-focused analysis of data collected to teach, monitor, and make predictions about children. [Official report](https://www.unicef.org/globalinsight/reports/governance-student-data)

## S028

Ma et al. (2014), “Intelligent Tutoring Systems and Learning Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis.” Examines overall outcomes and moderators including tutor type, comparison, outcome, and knowledge type. [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1049508)

## S029

Anthropic (2026), “The new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 generation models.” Product guidance advocating lighter global context, model judgment, progressive disclosure, simple tool descriptions, and rich references. Used only for corpus architecture. [Official article](https://claude.com/blog/the-new-rules-of-context-engineering-for-claude-5-generation-models)

## S030

American Academy of Pediatrics (2016), guidance accompanying “Media and Young Minds.” Recommends avoiding most media under 18 months and emphasizes high-quality content and caregiver co-use for young children. [AAP summary](https://publications.aap.org/aapnews/news/12960/Policy-addresses-how-to-help-parents-manage-young)

## S031

“Learning flow in educational contexts: A meta-analysis of its antecedents and outcomes” (2026). Integrates motivational and affective frameworks and explicitly notes that mostly correlational primary studies identify a nomological network rather than causal effects. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41815075/)

## S032

Fong, Zaleski, and Leach (2015), “The challenge–skill balance and antecedents of flow: A meta-analytic investigation.” Tests a central flow antecedent across 28 studies and finds the relationship meaningful but not sufficient as a universal prescription. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2014.967799)
