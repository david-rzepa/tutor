# Worked example: age-11 beginner French

This synthetic case tests the design principles; it is not a personal learner record. The learner is age 11, has guardian authorization, and wants usable beginner French. Actual onboarding must discover goals, prior exposure, access needs, device context, and preferred real-world topics.

## Target frame

Use the CEFR Companion Volume as a capability vocabulary, especially Pre-A1/A1, action-oriented learning, mediation, interaction, and uneven proficiency profiles. Do not reduce the curriculum to a single global level.

Candidate first authentic outcome:

> In a short, supported interaction, introduce oneself, understand and answer basic personal-preference questions, and repair one misunderstanding using safe fictional details.

Evidence should sample listening, spoken interaction, intelligible pronunciation, reading, and brief writing separately. A learner may be stronger in one; the profile retains the vector rather than averaging it away.

## Example capability slice

| Capability | Prerequisites | Teaching/practice | Mastery evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize and use greetings appropriate to context | none | short audio/video contrasts; caregiver/peer role-play | choose and produce a fitting greeting in novel scenes |
| Ask/answer name safely with a fictional persona | greeting turn-taking | model dialogue, substitution, prompt fading | unaided two-turn exchange with changed speaker/cue |
| Express simple likes/dislikes | high-frequency nouns/verbs as needed | meaningful choice, retrieval, picture/object prompts | respond and ask back across listening and speaking |
| Repair misunderstanding | basic phrases such as repetition/clarification requests | worked examples then role-play with deliberate noise/ambiguity | select and use a repair phrase in an unexpected breakdown |

Never solicit or publish real full name, school, location, schedule, contact details, or other identifying content for practice. Synthetic personas are sufficient.

## Flow-aware session sketch

1. Show one goal: complete a 30–60 second fictional greeting exchange.
2. Diagnose two or three prerequisites with low-stakes prompts.
3. Model only the missing part, then let the learner choose a fictional persona/topic.
4. Run short attempts with immediate, focused feedback and quick retries.
5. Raise challenge by varying voice, order, or context—not by simply speeding up.
6. End before fatigue with one visible success and one scheduled retrieval target.
7. Recheck later with a new speaker/context and without displayed language.

## Language-specific design rules

- Treat comprehensible, meaningful interaction and production as core; explicit form work supports rather than replaces use.
- Correct selectively: prioritize errors that block meaning, match the current target, or recur. Mix prompts and explicit/metalinguistic feedback; avoid correcting every utterance.
- Teach sound perception and production with audio exemplars, articulatory cues where useful, accessible playback controls, and human review paths; text alone cannot establish listening/pronunciation.
- Use retrieval and spacing for vocabulary, but embed words in varied communicative contexts and test both recognition and production.
- Include culture and pragmatics without stereotyping a single “French” identity.

## Generalization probes

The architecture must also explain how the same capability becomes:

- a caregiver-led naming/song/activity for a preschool learner rather than a solo chat;
- a captioned, keyboard-accessible route for a learner who cannot use audio or speech for a non-auditory objective;
- a faster, purpose-specific route for an adult with prior Romance-language knowledge;
- a lower-sensory-load interaction without lowering the language mastery target.
