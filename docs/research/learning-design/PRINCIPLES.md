# Instructional design principles

This file holds compact, executable principles. It is not a lesson script.

## P01 — Make retrieval do teaching work

- **Claim:** Attempting to retrieve learned material can improve later retention more than additional exposure, especially when attempts receive corrective feedback and recur after spacing.
- **Population/context:** Strongest evidence spans verbal and educational materials, with substantial school and adult evidence; exact benefits depend on task, prior learning, feedback, delay, and outcome.
- **Grade/basis:** `strong`; controlled testing-effect studies and broad reviews rank practice testing among the most generally useful learning techniques.
- **Design action:** After initial encoding, ask the learner to produce an answer before revealing it. Accept partial knowledge, diagnose the error, give concise explanatory feedback, and schedule a later attempt. Vary cues and require application when transfer matters.
- **Adaptation:** Reduce output and memory demands when they are not the target (for example, speech, selection, or assistive input), but preserve an effortful act of recall. For novices, precede retrieval with a worked example or scaffold.
- **Failure mode:** Quizzing unfamiliar material, revealing answers too early, rewarding guessing, repeating identical cues, or measuring only immediate success can create frustration or an illusion of mastery.
- **Signals:** Unaided delayed recall, transfer to a changed cue/task, error recurrence, response confidence calibration, attempts-to-mastery, and time-to-mastery at a fixed criterion.
- **Sources:** [S001](SOURCES.md#s001), [S002](SOURCES.md#s002).

## P02 — Space for the retention horizon

- **Claim:** Distributing practice across time generally improves long-term retention relative to massing it; the useful gap depends on how long the knowledge must last.
- **Grade/basis:** `strong`; quantitative synthesis across verbal learning tasks plus broad review.
- **Design action:** Revisit a skill after increasing, outcome-informed delays. Retrieve before restudy, reset conservatively after errors, and include delayed checks beyond the teaching session.
- **Failure mode:** A fixed schedule ignores item difficulty, prior knowledge, forgetting, desired retention interval, and missed sessions; “due-card completion” becomes the objective.
- **Signals:** Recall probability by delay, relearning cost, overdue recovery, delayed transfer, attempts/time to stable mastery.
- **Sources:** [S001](SOURCES.md#s001), [S004](SOURCES.md#s004).

## P03 — Interleave when discrimination is part of the skill

- **Claim:** Mixing related categories or problem types can improve later discrimination and inductive learning, but effects vary sharply by material and similarity.
- **Grade/basis:** `moderate`; meta-analysis finds meaningful moderators and no universal advantage.
- **Design action:** Establish minimal competence first, then mix confusable cases and require the learner to identify which strategy applies. Explain why practice feels harder.
- **Failure mode:** Random mixing before a usable schema exists raises load without teaching the contrast; mixing unrelated facts adds switching cost.
- **Signals:** Strategy/category selection on novel mixed items, confusion matrix, delayed transfer—not blocked-practice speed.
- **Sources:** [S005](SOURCES.md#s005).

## P04 — Show, fade, then vary worked examples

- **Claim:** Novices often learn complex procedures more efficiently from worked examples than from unsupported problem solving; support can reverse in value as expertise grows.
- **Grade/basis:** `moderate`; robust cognitive-load and worked-example literature with domain and expertise moderators.
- **Design action:** Model a correct solution with rationale, prompt self-explanation, convert steps to completion problems, then remove support and vary surface features.
- **Failure mode:** Passive example consumption, unexplained steps, permanent scaffolds, or examples mismatched to prior knowledge create imitation without transfer.
- **Signals:** Independent completion, explanation quality, error localization, performance on structurally similar but superficially changed tasks.
- **Sources:** [S006](SOURCES.md#s006).

## P05 — Feedback must change the next attempt

- **Claim:** Feedback is not uniformly beneficial; useful feedback identifies the task/process gap and supplies an actionable next move without replacing learner thinking.
- **Grade/basis:** `moderate`; syntheses show effects vary by timing, content, learner, and outcome.
- **Design action:** Acknowledge what is correct, locate the smallest consequential gap, give a hint/explanation proportional to need, and require a new attempt. Separate error correction from person-level praise.
- **Failure mode:** Giving the answer, vague praise, excessive correction, delayed feedback after misconceptions consolidate, or immediate interruption during productive reasoning.
- **Signals:** Error correction on retry, recurrence after delay, help level needed, transfer, learner calibration.
- **Sources:** [S007](SOURCES.md#s007), [S023](SOURCES.md#s023).

## P06 — Engineer conditions for flow, not compulsion

- **Claim:** Learning flow is associated with performance and motivation, and clear goals, usable feedback, focused attention, autonomy, and a high challenge–skill balance are recurring antecedents. Much of the educational evidence is correlational, so flow is a design hypothesis and secondary outcome—not proof of learning.
- **Grade/basis:** `moderate` association; `emerging` causal prescription. Recent meta-analysis explicitly warns that most contributing studies are correlational.
- **Design action:** Keep one meaningful proximal goal visible; remove irrelevant interruption; tune the next task from demonstrated capability; provide fast informational feedback; offer bounded choice; preserve continuity across hints and retries. If errors accelerate or latency/frustration rises, reduce one source of difficulty or add a scaffold. If responses become automatic without learning value, increase meaningful variation or advance.
- **Failure mode:** Optimizing streaks, session length, points, urgency, or endless difficulty adjustment can create compulsive engagement, hide fatigue, and displace retention or real-world activity. A challenge exactly matched to current skill can also cease producing growth.
- **Signals:** Optional brief flow check, interruption/reorientation cost, challenge and confidence reports, hint/error/latency trajectories, voluntary return—always interpreted alongside delayed retention, transfer, well-being, and stopping behavior.
- **Sources:** [S031](SOURCES.md#s031), [S032](SOURCES.md#s032).

## P07 — Teach self-regulation and support agency

- **Claim:** Planning, monitoring, evaluating, autonomy support, competence, and relatedness can support learning and motivation; young learners need these strategies taught and scaffolded rather than assumed.
- **Grade/basis:** `moderate`; meta-analyses of primary-school self-regulation training, computer-based metacognitive prompts, and self-determination interventions.
- **Design action:** Ask for a plan or prediction, elicit confidence, compare it with performance, and close with one reflection or next strategy. Offer meaningful choices within a coherent route and explain adaptation.
- **Failure mode:** Reflection overhead interrupts flow; unlimited choice overloads novices; praise or rewards become controlling.
- **Signals:** Calibration, strategy transfer, help-seeking quality, independent planning, mastery, voluntary persistence.
- **Sources:** [S011](SOURCES.md#s011), [S012](SOURCES.md#s012).

## P08 — Advance on uncertain mastery, then verify retention and transfer

- **Claim:** Mastery learning can improve achievement, but a single correctness threshold is not ground truth. Model uncertainty and require evidence across time and contexts.
- **Grade/basis:** `strong` for mastery-learning programs broadly; `emerging` for any particular adaptive threshold.
- **Design action:** Define mastery before teaching. Combine multiple independent opportunities, acceptable support level, delayed retention, and transfer; advance provisionally when evidence is sufficient and schedule reconfirmation.
- **Failure mode:** Repeated near-identical items inflate confidence; a universal 0.95 model threshold launders model assumptions into truth; overpractice wastes time.
- **Signals:** Posterior uncertainty, independent opportunities, retention, transfer, relearning cost, false-mastery and overpractice rates.
- **Sources:** [S008](SOURCES.md#s008), [S019](SOURCES.md#s019).

## P09 — Put a pedagogical harness around generative AI

- **Claim:** AI tutoring can improve outcomes in some bounded settings, while unguarded answer-generating access can reduce unaided learning. The instructional harness and content grounding matter at least as much as model fluency.
- **Grade/basis:** `emerging`; growing randomized field evidence with narrow populations, subjects, durations, and implementations.
- **Design action:** Ground to approved curriculum; preserve productive learner work; prefer hints, questions, diagnosis, examples, and feedback before answers; validate generated tasks/claims; expose uncertainty; test unaided learning; provide human escalation.
- **Failure mode:** Hallucination, answer dependence, sycophancy, false confidence, unsafe content, hidden persuasion, and optimizing assisted performance.
- **Signals:** Unaided delayed and transfer tests, factual error/hallucination audit, answer-reveal rate, learner work ratio, escalation outcomes, subgroup effects.
- **Sources:** [S020](SOURCES.md#s020), [S021](SOURCES.md#s021), [S022](SOURCES.md#s022).

## Explicit anti-principle — Do not assign fixed learning styles

- **Claim:** Learners have preferences and real accessibility needs, but evidence does not justify matching instruction to a diagnosed visual/auditory/kinesthetic “style” as a general route to better learning.
- **Grade/basis:** `unsupported` for the matching (“meshing”) hypothesis.
- **Design action:** Match representation to the content and access need; offer useful modality choices; infer adaptation from observed outcomes. Never turn a preference into a permanent capability label.
- **Failure mode:** A profile label narrows opportunities, hides a disability or prerequisite gap, and spends adaptation budget on an unsupported interaction.
- **Signals:** Compare delayed mastery across accessible representations rather than preference satisfaction alone.
- **Sources:** [S003](SOURCES.md#s003).
