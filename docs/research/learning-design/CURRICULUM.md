# Curriculum architecture

A curriculum is a versioned capability graph plus evidence rules, not a playlist. Sequence from target performance and prerequisites while allowing multiple routes.

## Core entities

### Capability

- stable ID and version;
- observable “learner can…” outcome;
- domain/context and intended population;
- prerequisite capabilities and common misconceptions;
- acceptable evidence, minimum independence, retention interval, and transfer demand;
- accessibility-preserving alternatives;
- source/provenance and reviewer.

### Learning activity

- capabilities taught and assessed;
- mechanism: example, retrieval, discrimination, explanation, project/task, dialogue, simulation, etc.;
- difficulty dimensions, expected time, and cognitive/access demands;
- assistance ladder and feedback rules;
- answer/rubric plus validation status;
- age/content/safety constraints;
- variants that change surface form without changing the construct.

### Evidence event

- capability, item/activity version, timestamp, context, response, independence/help, confidence, latency bounds, result, and evaluator provenance;
- uncertainty and possible alternative explanation;
- privacy classification and retention/deletion class.

## Backward-design sequence

1. Define authentic target performances and fixed mastery criteria.
2. Decompose only as far as separately observable prerequisites and misconceptions require.
3. Create diagnostic items that sample prerequisites without turning onboarding into an exam.
4. For each new capability: connect to prior knowledge, model for novices, fade support, retrieve, vary, interleave confusable cases, and schedule delayed/transfer evidence.
5. Revisit capabilities spirally in richer contexts; do not repeat identical content under a new label.
6. Provide remediation routes by error hypothesis, not a generic “repeat lesson.”

## Sequencing policy

Choose the next activity by constrained expected value:

1. satisfy safety, authority, accessibility, time, and prerequisite constraints;
2. prioritize at-risk prerequisites and due retention checks;
3. prefer the activity expected to reduce decision-relevant mastery uncertainty or produce authentic transfer;
4. among educationally equivalent choices, prefer the one likely to sustain healthy flow and learner agency;
5. reserve some learner choice and exploration without abandoning coherent progression.

Do not maximize predicted correctness: that selects easy work. Do not maximize difficulty: that selects struggle. Target informative, meaningful success with enough challenge to require learning.

## Mastery evidence contract

Use multiple opportunities and represent uncertainty. Evidence should include:

- independent performance after scaffolding is removed;
- delayed retrieval at a declared interval;
- transfer to a changed cue/context where the goal requires it;
- misconception-sensitive items, not only recognition;
- representative modalities for the target capability;
- no access barrier mistaken for lack of knowledge.

A threshold is a policy choice to validate, not a psychological constant. Permit provisional advancement with scheduled reconfirmation, and detect both false mastery and wasteful overpractice.
